Homework 11 — Cross-Team SRS Review

Group → groupLesson 10Submit: Review report to the other team + revised SRS

Objective

Do a real static review of a real document written by people who are not in the room — and find out what your own document looks like to a stranger.

Task

  1. Teams are paired up. You review the other team's SRS; they review yours. Both reviews happen at the same time.
  2. Preparation — read the whole document individually first, without commenting. Note where you had to guess what something meant.
  3. Review against a checklist:
  4. • Complete — is anything obviously missing? Are there requirements for every feature described in the scope?
    • Unambiguous — could two developers read a requirement and build different things?
    • Testable — can you write a test case with one expected result from each requirement? Try it on three of them.
    • SMART — specific, measurable, achievable, realistic, timely.
    • Consistent — do any two requirements contradict each other? Is the same term used for the same thing throughout?
    • NFRs — are performance, security, usability, availability and accessibility covered with numbers?
    • Architecture — does the diagram match the requirements?
    • Test cases — does every functional requirement have at least one, and do they have measurable expected results?
  5. Write the review report: one row per finding — location, what is wrong, which checklist item it breaks, severity (blocking / should fix / suggestion), and a suggested rewrite for at least the blocking ones.
  6. Hold the review meeting — 20 minutes with the other team. You present your findings; they ask questions. Comment on the document, never on the people. Then swap roles.
  7. Follow-up — each team revises their own SRS based on the review received, and records which findings they accepted, which they rejected, and why.

Deliverable

A review report handed to the other team, plus your own revised SRS and a short list of accepted and rejected findings with reasons.

Assessment criteria

CriterionWhat we look for
Real findingsSubstantive issues found, not only typos and formatting
Testability checkYou actually tried to write test cases from their requirements and reported where it failed
SeverityBlocking issues separated from suggestions
ConstructiveEvery blocking finding comes with a suggested rewrite
Follow-upYour own SRS improved, and rejected findings are argued rather than ignored
